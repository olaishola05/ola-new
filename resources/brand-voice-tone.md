## WHAT IS VOICE VS TONE?

**Voice** is constant. It is who you are when you communicate. It does not change across platforms or content types.

**Tone** shifts depending on context. You are the same person but you speak differently in a classroom than at a dinner table. Your tone adjusts. Your voice stays the same.

## 10. VOCABULARY AND LANGUAGE PREFERENCES

*Small word choices add up to a consistent voice over time.*

**Preferred words and phrases:**
- "Build" over "create" when talking about products and systems
- "Ship" when talking about launching or releasing something
- "Learner" over "student" when referring to people in your school
- "Founder" over "entrepreneur" for people building products
- "System" over "process" when talking about workflows
- "Practical" over "simple" when describing your teaching approach
- "Real" as an emphasis word: real products, real results, real problems

**Words to avoid:**
- "Guru" or "ninja" or "rockstar" as developer descriptors
- "Hustle" used approvingly
- "Disruptive" used without substance
- "Leverage" used lazily without meaning
- "Simply" or "just" before an instruction (it minimises difficulty and alienates beginners)
- "Obviously" or "clearly" (condescending)
- Excessive use of "amazing" or "awesome" as filler praise

**Sentence preferences:**
- Active voice over passive voice always
- Contractions are welcome: "you will" can be "you will" but "it's" and "that's" are fine in conversational content
- Numbers over words for steps: "3 things" not "three things"

## 11. SENTENCE LENGTH AND RHYTHM GUIDE

*Your natural writing rhythm is one of your strongest voice assets. Preserve it deliberately.*

**The pattern:**
You naturally alternate between short declarative sentences and longer explanatory ones. The short sentence lands the point. The longer sentence explains it, adds context, or tells the story behind it. Then another short sentence closes or pivots. This rhythm creates momentum and readability without sacrificing depth.

**Example of the rhythm in practice:**
"Validation is not about catching errors. It is about building trust with the person using your product. When you skip it or treat it as an afterthought, you are essentially telling your user that you did not think about them. That is a problem. Not just technically. Personally."

**Rules to follow:**
- Never write more than three long sentences in a row without a short one to break the rhythm
- A paragraph longer than five lines should be split
- The most important sentence in any paragraph should be the shortest
- End sections with a short, clear, forward-pointing sentence
